## [0:00–0:25] HOOK — your laptop is losing value. Theirs isn't.

Every computer you have ever owned lost value the day you bought it. The CPU got faster, the price got lower, and your machine slid toward the recycling bin.

Now meet the one network that runs that logic in reverse. On RustChain, a PowerBook G4 from 2003 earns **2.5 times** more than a modern Threadripper. Your hardware doesn't depreciate here — it *appreciates* as it ages. Start mining today at 0.8x, keep the machine alive for ten years, and your multiplier grows with it.

This is Proof-of-Antiquity: a blockchain that rewards hardware for *surviving*, not for being fast.

## [0:25–1:20] PROBLEM — proof-of-work burns the planet, proof-of-stake rewards the rich

Bitcoin made mining a race for electricity. Ethereum's merge made it a race for capital — whoever holds the most stake earns the most. Both systems punish the little guy: either you buy warehouse-scale power or you buy a mountain of tokens.

And both ignore a mountain of a different kind: e-waste. Fifty-plus million tonnes of dead electronics a year, while perfectly working twenty-year-old machines get shredded because no protocol values them.

RustChain asks a different question: what if the rarest thing in computing isn't speed, but *provenance*? What if you could prove — with physics, not promises — that a specific twenty-year-old machine is real, alive, and doing work right now?

## [1:20–2:30] MECHANISM — six checks no VM can fake

Every RustChain miner passes a hardware fingerprint: six independent checks — oscillator drift, cache timing across L1/L2/L3, SIMD identity, thermal entropy, instruction jitter, and anti-emulation. A SheepShaver VM pretending to be a G4 fails, because real vintage silicon carries unique aging patterns that can't be faked.

The attestation server doesn't trust self-reported data. It verifies the physics, then weights your reward shares by your device's antiquity multiplier: PowerPC G4 from 2003 at **2.5x**, modern ARM NAS boards at a **0.0005x** penalty tier because they're cheap and farmable.

The network runs on live attestation nodes — two always-on in Louisiana plus a lab IBM POWER8 S824 with 512 GB of RAM — and anyone can watch: twenty-plus miners attesting, queryable right now with one curl against `https://rustchain.org/api/miners`. Your own balance and multiplier are one more curl away.

## [2:30–3:20] STAKES — fixed money, real machines, agent identity

The money is fixed: **8,388,608 RTC total** — two to the twenty-third, pure binary, consensus-enforced cap, forever. Compare Bitcoin's 21 million. The small cap is deliberate: it forces the economy to discover real value per token instead of printing its way out.

And the timing matters beyond mining. For AI teams, this is Sybil-resistant agent authentication: an agent running on a verified POWER8 server is *provably* different from one on a Raspberry Pi, because the fingerprint is silicon-level. Bot swarms and VM farms fingerprint as what they are. Media in the ecosystem can even carry AVAP — agents cryptographically signing messages inside the videos they exchange.

RustChain already serves as the attestation chain for a live U.S. legal-corpus determinism engagement run by Elyan Labs — over a million units of federal and state material, verified deterministic.

## [3:20–4:00] CLOSE — time is undefeated

Your brand-new Threadripper will be vintage hardware someday. Your M4 MacBook will be a museum piece. That RTX 5090 will be a curiosity. Time is undefeated — and on this network, time pays.

If it runs Python, it can mine: Linux across x86_64, ppc64le, aarch64, mips, sparc, m68k, riscv64, plus macOS, IBM POWER8, and Windows. Dig the old machine out of the closet, attest it, and watch the multiplier do what no hardware has ever done before — grow.
